INCIDENT RESPONSE PLAN: NIST SP 800-61 ALIGNED1. Background, Purpose, and Scope1.1 BackgroundRecent proactive security monitoring identified anomalous network traffic and unauthorized credential usage pointing to a potential security breach involving confidential organizational data. In response to this event, this Incident Response Plan (IRP) has been activated to ensure a structured, rapid, and effective response to contain the threat and minimize organizational impact.1.2 PurposeThe purpose of this Incident Response Plan is to establish a standardized process for detecting, analyzing, containing, eradicating, and recovering from cybersecurity incidents. This plan aligns directly with the guidelines set forth in NIST Special Publication 800-61 Revision 2 (Computer Security Incident Handling Guide).1.3 ScopeThis plan applies to all information systems, networks, endpoint devices, cloud services, and personnel within the organization. It governs the handling of all security incidents, ranging from low-priority policy violations to major critical data breaches.2. Phase 1: PreparationPreparation forms the foundation of incident response. The organization maintains readiness through the following measures:Infrastructure & Tools: Deployment of SIEM (Security Information and Event Management), EDR (Endpoint Detection and Response), secure logging servers, and forensic toolkits.Documentation: Maintenance of updated network diagrams, system baselines, asset inventories, and call trees.Training & Exercises: Conducting annual cybersecurity awareness training for all staff and periodic tabletop exercises for the Incident Response Team (IRT).Phase 2: Detection & AnalysisWhen anomalous activity is flagged, the team immediately executes the following steps:Identification: Collect alerts from SIEM, EDR, network firewalls, and user reports.Triage & Categorization: Validate whether the alert is a false positive or a true incident. Classify severity based on system criticality and data sensitivity (e.g., Low, Medium, High, Critical).Scope & Impact Analysis: Determine which assets, systems, and user accounts are affected. Identify potential indicators of compromise (IoCs) such as malicious IP addresses, file hashes, or suspicious registry modifications.Documentation: Log all timeline events, actions taken, and evidence gathered in an immutable Incident Management System.Phase 3: Containment, Eradication & RecoveryThis phase focuses on limiting damage and safely restoring operational capability.Short-Term Containment:Isolate affected network segments and compromised hosts from the local network and internet.Disable compromised user and service accounts in Active Directory/Identity Provider.Block malicious IP addresses and domains at the perimeter firewall/proxy.Long-Term Containment:Apply temporary patches or workaround configurations to prevent immediate reinfection.Issue clean, temporary credentials and infrastructure where necessary.Eradication:Remove all traces of malware, unauthorized backdoors, and persistence mechanisms across all systems.Re-image compromised endpoint devices using known-good system backups.Remediate root-cause vulnerabilities (e.g., apply security patches, reconfigure misconfigured services).Recovery:Restore affected systems from verified, clean backups.Validate system integrity and monitor enhanced logging before reintroducing systems to production.Gradually restore full functionality while continuously monitoring network traffic for secondary anomalies.Phase 4: Post-Incident ActivityPost-incident handling ensures continuous improvement of security controls and response strategies:Hold a formal Lessons Learned meeting within 5-10 operational days of incident closure.Analyze root causes, response timeliness, tool effectiveness, and process bottlenecks.Archive all incident records and forensic evidence securely for legal and compliance requirements.3. Team Roles and Responsibilities3.1 Key Incident Response RolesRolePrimary ResponsibilitiesIncident Commander (IC)Overall lead during an incident. Coordinates all technical and operational response efforts, approves major containment decisions, and manages escalate pathways to executive leadership.Technical Specialist / Forensic AnalystPerforms deep-dive investigation, log analysis, malware analysis, memory forensics, and technical containment/eradication execution.Communications LeadManages internal employee updates and external PR/media communications to ensure consistent, approved messaging.Legal & Compliance OfficerAdvises on legal risks, regulatory disclosure requirements (e.g., GDPR, HIPAA), and handles interactions with law enforcement if necessary.IT Infrastructure / Systems AdminAssists with system isolation, active directory management, server re-imaging, firewall updates, and operational recovery.3.2 Responsibility Transfer and EscalationHand-offs: Shift hand-overs during extended incidents require a formal brief covering incident status, current tasks, and unassigned work items.Escalation Path: If an incident escalates from Low/Medium to High/Critical, the Incident Commander immediately notifies Executive Leadership and activates the Legal and Communications leads.4. Post-Incident Analysis & Lessons Learned4.1 Post-Incident Review ProcessFollowing incident resolution, the Incident Commander convenes a post-incident review session with key stakeholders to address the following key questions:Exactly what happened, and at what times?How well did staff and management perform in following the incident response plan?Were documented procedures adequate, and were they followed correctly?Which indicators or alerts led to the detection of the incident?What preventative actions could have stopped the incident?4.2 Updating Policies, Procedures, and TrainingPolicy & Playbook Updates: Update technical playbooks and firewall rules within 14 days of the post-incident meeting based on gaps identified.Security Controls Enhancement: Implement additional technical controls (e.g., strict Multi-Factor Authentication, zero-trust network policies) to block identified attack vectors.Training Adjustments: Incorporate real-world scenario details into upcoming employee security awareness training to address specific vectors (e.g., tailored phishing awareness).5. ConclusionA well-structured, NIST SP 800-61 aligned Incident Response Plan is essential for maintaining operational resilience and protecting sensitive organizational assets. Swift containment and structured recovery drastically reduce potential financial, operational, and reputational damage.However, an incident response plan is a dynamic document. To remain effective against evolving cyber threats, this plan must be subjected to regular testing—such as bi-annual tabletop exercises and simulation tests—and updated at least annually or immediately following any major security incident.6.
